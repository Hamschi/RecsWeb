Ingredients

  • 1.5kg beef marrow bones
  • 2l water
  • 2tbsp salt
  • 2tbsp vinegar
  • 600g beef for soup
  • water
  • 0.5tsp salt
  • 2small pieces ginger
  • 2pcs shallots
  • 0.5tsp salt
  • 4l water
  • 0.5tbsp salt
  • 10g rock sugar
  • 60g ginger
  • 1pcs onion (big)
  • 6g cinnamon
  • 3pcs star anise
  • 7pcs cloves
  • 5g mandarin peel (dried, optional)
  • 5g coriander seeds
  • 10g white pepper
  • 3pcs black cardamom pods
  • 3g cumin seeds
  • 22g salt
  • 48g chicken bouillon powder
  • 45g rock sugar
  • 13g MSG
  • phở noodles
  • 600g beef for serving
  • cilantro
  • spring onions
  • onion
  • chili (optional)
  • mung bean sprouts (optional)
  • lime (optional)
  • chili sauce (optional)
  • hoisin sauce (optional)

Instructions

  1. Soak the beef bones in 2 liters of water with 2 tbsp of salt and 2 tbsp of vinegar for 2 hours.
  2. Rinse the bones thoroughly several times under running water.
  3. Slice 2 small pieces of ginger and 2 shallots.
  4. Blanch 600 g of beef for 2 minutes in plenty of water with 0.5 tsp of salt and half of the ginger and shallots.
  5. Rinse the beef thoroughly under running water.
  6. Place the beef marrow bones in a large pot with plenty of water, 0.5 tsp of salt and the remaining ginger and shallots.
  7. Bring the water to a boil over high heat and boil for 5 minutes.
  8. Turn off the heat and rinse the bones thoroughly under running water.
  9. Place the bones and beef in a large pot with 0.5 tbsp of salt and 10 g of rock sugar. Add 4 liters of water and bring to a boil. Skim off any foam that rises to the surface.
  10. Remove the beef once tender, after about 2 hours, and place it in cold water for 10 minutes. Continue simmering the broth for a total of 6–8 hours.
  11. After 3.5 hours, wrap 60 g of ginger and 1 large onion in aluminum foil and roast at 190°C for about 15 minutes.
  12. Meanwhile, heat a dry pan and toast the larger spices: cinnamon, star anise, black cardamom and dried mandarin peel.
  13. After 30 seconds, add the smaller spices: cloves, cumin seeds and coriander seeds.
  14. Once the spices become fragrant, place them all in a tea infuser.
  15. Fill a large soup bowl with boiling water, add the tea infuser and let the spices steep until needed.
  16. Once the ginger and onion have softened in the oven, peel, wash and crush the ginger. Peel the onion and make cuts from the top as if quartering it.
  17. After the broth has simmered for 4 hours in total, add the roasted onion and ginger.
  18. After another 90 minutes, add the tea infuser containing the spices to the broth.
  19. After another 40 minutes, remove the tea infuser and add water until there are 4 liters of liquid again.
  20. Remove the bones from the broth.
  21. Mix 22 g of salt, 48 g of chicken bouillon powder, 45 g of rock sugar, 13 g of MSG and 3 ladles of broth. Add the mixture to the pot.
  22. Optionally, continue simmering the broth for up to 2 more hours.
  23. About 30 minutes before the broth is finished, prepare the remaining ingredients.
  24. Prepare the pho noodles according to the package instructions.
  25. Cut a fresh onion into thin strips and place them in ice water.
  26. Cut the dark green parts of the spring onions into very thin rings. Cut the white parts into very thin strips. Roughly cut or chop the cilantro.
  27. Cut the limes into wedges that are easy to squeeze.
  28. Cut the cooked beef into very thin slices.
  29. Cut the raw beef into very thin slices.
  30. Place the pho noodles, both parts of the spring onions, cilantro, fresh onion and both types of beef in serving bowls. Pour the piping-hot pho broth over everything.
  31. Serve with chili sauce, hoisin sauce, mung bean sprouts or lime juice to taste.
  32. The raw beef should cook through in the hot pho broth. If preferred, place the beef in a fine-mesh strainer and hold it in the simmering broth until completely cooked before serving.
